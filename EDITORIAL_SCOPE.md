# United States Travel & Wonder Guide — Editorial Scope

The guide is intentionally broader than a national-park directory.

National parks absolutely belong here, and the long-term inventory should include all U.S. national parks because they are among the country's most significant travel-and-wonder destinations. But they are only one subset of the places this guide is meant to help people discover. Excellent dedicated national-park resources already exist; this project's value is in putting national parks alongside the many other places that can justify a trip, reshape a route, or reveal something extraordinary about a state.

## What belongs in the guide

Potential Wonders can include, among other things:

- National parks, monuments, preserves, lakeshores, seashores, recreation areas, and other NPS units
- State parks and state natural areas
- National forests and grasslands
- National wildlife refuges
- BLM and other public-land destinations
- Wilderness areas
- Scenic byways and remarkable road corridors
- Coastlines, beaches, dunes, islands, lakes, rivers, waterfalls, canyons, mountains, deserts, caves, springs, forests, and geological features
- Wildlife-viewing destinations
- Dark-sky destinations
- Gardens, preserves, arboretums, and distinctive landscapes
- Historic, cultural, architectural, engineering, and industrial places when the place itself creates a strong travel/wonder experience
- Small roadside or regional places that are genuinely worth the detour even when they are not nationally famous

The guide should not privilege a destination merely because it is federally designated or widely marketed.

## National parks are a subset, not the organizing principle

National parks should be included because they qualify on merit, not because the guide is built around the National Park System.

As the inventory expands, editorial review should actively watch for over-concentration in any one agency, designation type, or travel style. A state's Signature Wonder may be a national park, but its ◆ Worth Traveling For and ○ Worth the Detour entries should emerge from the strongest places in that state regardless of who manages them.

## Editorial test

A candidate belongs when the place itself gives a traveler a compelling reason to see it and fits one of the guide's three trip-shaping roles:

- **★ Signature Wonder** — the one place that best makes the case for the state.
- **◆ Worth Traveling For** — strong enough to justify building part of a trip around it.
- **○ Worth the Detour** — worth changing or extending a route to experience when nearby.

Designation, popularity, and visitor count are evidence to consider, not automatic qualification.

## Diversity guardrail for expansion

Before a state is considered reasonably developed, review whether its entries represent the actual range of wonder available there. That may include a mix of federal and state land, urban and rural places, natural and cultural destinations, highly accessible stops and remote destinations, famous anchors and lesser-known discoveries.

Do not force diversity numerically, but do not let the easiest-to-research category become the de facto scope of the guide.
