# Phase 2 Research Method

Phase 2 separates discovery research from publishing so the guide does not become a quota-driven list or drift into being a national-park directory.

## Phase 2A — Research pool

A place may enter the candidate pool before it has a final reason-to-go classification. Candidate research should answer four questions:

1. **Is it genuinely travel-worthy?** Would the place itself give a traveler a reason to go, alter a route, or make meaningful time for it?
2. **Is it distinct?** Does it add a materially different experience from the area's existing Signature Wonder and other candidates?
3. **Is it realistically visitable?** Can a normal traveler reasonably access and experience it, even if reservations, ferries, rough roads, permits, seasons, or guided access require planning?
4. **Can it be supported responsibly?** Is there a reliable managing, official, tribal, municipal, state, federal, nonprofit stewardship, or authoritative destination source for core facts and planning?

## Research status

Phase 2A uses these review statuses:

- `strong` — clearly belongs in the Phase 2B classification pool.
- `uncertain` — potentially valuable, but needs comparison, access review, or stronger sourcing before classification.
- `overlap` — valid place, but may duplicate another candidate or represent a broad region better handled through a more specific destination.
- `defer` — currently a poor production candidate because access, closure, operational uncertainty, or another material issue prevents responsible publication now.
- `not-fit` — does not meet the guide's travel-and-wonder editorial threshold.

These statuses are **not tiers**. A `strong` candidate still has not been assigned ◆ Worth Traveling For or ○ Worth the Detour.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. managing agency / land manager / tribal authority;
2. state, territorial, municipal, or federal official destination page;
3. official stewardship organization for a privately or nonprofit-managed destination;
4. official destination marketing organization when no single land manager represents a regional place;
5. reputable secondary sources only for comparative context, never as the sole authority for access restrictions or operational facts.

## Travel viability review

At Phase 2A, do not copy volatile details such as current fees or temporary closures into permanent candidate rationale. Instead flag planning characteristics that affect whether the place is a viable guide destination, such as:

- ferry, boat, tour, or guide dependency;
- permit or reservation dependency;
- tribal-land access requirements;
- seasonal road closures;
- rough, unpaved, steep, winding, or high-clearance roads;
- large-RV restrictions;
- extremely remote access;
- limited public access;
- an active closure that makes current publication misleading.

## Diversity check

Every area should be researched beyond the easiest federal-land options. Candidate pools should actively consider state/local parks, wildlife areas, scenic roads, regional landscapes, coastlines, islands, architecture, cultural and historic places, engineering works, tribal destinations, gardens, caves, waterfalls, dark-sky locations, and smaller places with genuine detour value.

National parks are tracked separately in `NATIONAL_PARKS_COVERAGE.md` so every national park receives eventual consideration without allowing NPS inventory to define the guide.

## Wave workflow

Phase 2A proceeds in research waves rather than publishing immediately:

1. broaden the candidate pool;
2. verify at least one authoritative source per reviewed candidate;
3. record a preliminary review status and concise reason;
4. compare the pool with the area's current ★ Signature Wonder;
5. flag likely Signature challenges without changing the live Signature yet;
6. move a sufficiently researched area into Phase 2B classification.

Phase 2C is the first point where newly approved candidates are turned into public Wonder records.
